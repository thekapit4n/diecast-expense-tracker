# Changes Log - 2026-08-30

This note records all work done today in simple language.

## Quick Overview

- New feature: you can now record a car **leaving** your collection — given away as a gift, sold, traded, lost or damaged. Until today the app only knew how to record buying.
- The car stays in the Catalog and the money you spent stays in "Total Spent". Only the owned count drops.
- Selling shows a live profit breakdown that includes postage and platform/COD fees, not just sale price minus purchase price.
- A gift never counts as a loss in the profit numbers.
- You can record it from the Catalog card as well as the Purchase List — easier when you're going by the photo.
- Gift and sale details are only visible when logged in; the public Catalog never shows who received a car.
- New sidebar section **Collection Changes**: an **Insight** page with charts, plus a page each for Sold, Gifted, Traded, Lost and Damaged — cards with car photos, a search box and infinite scroll.
- New dependency: **@amcharts/amcharts5** for the Insight charts.
- The Dashboard's **Spending by Brand** panel is now a sorted bar chart too, biggest spender on top, replacing the old progress-bar list.
- The mobile app now subtracts cars that have left too, so phone and web finally agree.

## What Changed

### 1) New database table — `tbl_disposal`

- The app used to work out what you own purely from the purchase record: paid, and collected if it was a pre-order. There was no way to say "I used to have this car, now I don't".
- The only workaround was deleting the purchase — but that also erased the money you actually spent, which is wrong. You really did spend it.
- So buying and leaving are now two separate records. The purchase row is never touched when a car leaves; a new "disposal" row is what removes it from your owned count.
- Every disposal answers two **separate** questions, and they don't depend on each other:
  - **Why did it leave?** — gift / sold / traded / lost / damaged
  - **How did you hand it over?** — face to face / posted / COD
- This matters because a gift handed over face to face and a sale handed over face to face are the same handover but report completely differently.

### 2) Two safety rules built into the database

- **You can't record more cars leaving than you bought.** If a purchase row is for 2 cars and 1 already left, the app blocks a third. This is enforced in the database itself, not just the screen.
- **You can't delete a purchase that has a gift or sale attached.** Deleting it would destroy the record of where the car went and any money it earned. The app now explains this instead of deleting.

### 3) "Car Left Collection" on the Purchase List

- Right-click a row in the Purchase List (on the item name column) and pick **Car Left Collection (Gift / Sold)**.
- The form only asks what's relevant, so you're not typing zeros into boxes you don't need:
  - **Face to face** — just how much you got. Nothing else. Cash and car change hands at the same moment.
  - **Posted / courier** — adds postage you paid and the platform fee.
  - **COD** — adds postage and the COD fee, plus a "money received?" question, because with COD the car leaves before the money arrives.
- Choosing **Gift** hides all the money boxes — a gift is RM 0 by definition.
- A new **Still Owned** column is available in the Purchase List (hidden by default, switch it on from the column menu). "Quantity" stays as what you bought; "Still Owned" is what's left after anything gifted or sold.

### 4) Live profit breakdown when selling

- The sale form shows the real maths as you type, because the obvious sum is misleading. For a car bought at RM 50 and sold at RM 80:

| | Simple guess | What the app shows |
|---|---|---|
| You got | RM 80 | RM 80 |
| Postage you paid | — | − RM 8 |
| Platform / COD fee | — | − RM 4 |
| You paid for the car | − RM 50 | − RM 50 |
| **Result** | **RM 30** | **RM 18** |

- Profit only counts once the money has actually arrived. A COD sale still waiting on the courier shows the car as gone, but doesn't count as profit yet — the same way a purchase isn't yours until it's paid.

### 5) Gifts are not losses

- A gift removes the car from your collection, but never appears in the profit report. Giving a present isn't a trading loss — treating it as one would show a fake RM 50 loss every time you're generous.
- The money you originally spent stays in "Total Spent" either way.

### 6) Owned counts updated across the app

- **Catalog** — a car you gave away now shows 0 owned and its tile goes back to looking like a car you don't have yet. The car itself stays in the Catalog; it's still a real model, you just don't own one.
- **Dashboard "Total Items"** — now subtracts cars that have left. "Total Spent" is deliberately left alone, because that money was really spent.
- The public Catalog page (viewable logged out) reads disposals too, so a car you gave away doesn't still show as owned to visitors.

### 7) Record a car leaving straight from the Catalog

- The Purchase List is a table of item numbers. The Catalog has the photos — and when you're trying to remember which car you gave away, the photo is what you recognise.
- So each purchase row inside a Catalog card now has a **Car left collection** button. It opens the same form as the Purchase List, and the card updates by itself once you save.
- The button only appears when you are logged in, and only on cars you still own. Once every unit of that purchase has gone, it disappears — there is nothing left to record.
- If you bought the same car twice, each purchase gets its own button, so you choose which one left.

### 8) Gift and sale details are private

- The Catalog is a public page — anyone with the link can open it, logged in or not.
- Recipient names and remarks are personal ("gift to Haziq's son"), so the badge and the whole detail line are shown **only when you are logged in**.
- A visitor still sees the correct owned count, because that is worked out on the server. They just never see who received a car, what it sold for, or your notes.

### 9) New menu section — "Collection Changes"

- Until now you could record a car leaving but never see the list again, and there was no way to undo a mistake. This section fixes that.
- A new group in the sidebar with one page per reason: **Sold**, **Gifted**, **Traded**, **Lost**, **Damaged**. Each page shows only that kind of change, so the Gifted page is your gift list and nothing else.
- Every record is a card with a **photo of the car**, so you can recognise which one it was without reading item numbers. The photo is the first one the app has for that car; if it can't find one it falls back to the next, and shows a placeholder if there are none.
- **Click the photo to enlarge it** — it opens full size over a dark background. Press Escape or the X to close.
- Each card also shows **what the car cost you in the first place**: `Paid RM 55.00 each · From Art of Speed Malaysia 2025 · On 26 Jul 2025`. This appears on all five pages, not just Sold — a gift record is meaningless without knowing the value of what you gave away. Multi-unit purchases also show what the whole row cost. A **Chase** tag appears next to the brand where it applies.
- **Search box** on every page — matches the car name, item number, brand, who got it, or your remark.
- **Infinite scroll** — 12 cards load first, more appear as you scroll. A "showing X of Y" line tells you where you are.
- Summary numbers sit at the top. Sold and Traded show profit after postage and fees, plus how many COD sales are still waiting for money. Gifted, Lost and Damaged just show a count — a gift has no profit, so showing one would be misleading.
- Two different ways to reverse a record, and the difference matters:
  - **Came back to me** — the buyer refused the parcel or returned it. The car goes back into your collection, but the postage you already wasted stays on record. Use this for a real return.
  - **Remove record** — you typed it in wrongly. Deletes the record completely, as if it never happened. Asks for confirmation first, because the money recorded against it is forgotten too.
- A returned record shows greyed out with a dashed border and a "Returned" tag, and can be marked gone again if it leaves a second time.

### 10) Insight page with charts

- First item in the Collection Changes section: **Insight**, an overview across all five reasons.
- Four summary numbers: cars gone, profit from sales, money taken (with how much of it went on postage and fees), and how many COD sales are still waiting.
- **Where the cars went** — a bar chart counting cars by reason, darkest bar for the biggest number. One shade of blue rather than five different colours, because the reasons are categories being counted, not five things being tracked over time.
- **Profit by month** — the last 6 months of settled sales. Profitable months point up in blue, losing months point down in red. Quiet months show as a gap rather than disappearing.
- Underneath both charts is a plain table of the same numbers, so nothing depends on being able to tell colours apart.
- Gifts never appear in the profit chart, and a COD still waiting for payment is left out until the money actually arrives — same rule as everywhere else.
- Charts are built with the **amCharts 5** library (newly added to the project).

### 11) Mobile app now agrees with the web app

- The mobile app worked out owned quantities on its own and knew nothing about cars leaving, so a car you gave away on the web still showed as owned on your phone.
- Mobile now subtracts cars that have left in all three places it counts them: the **Catalog** tiles, the **Dashboard** "units owned", and the **Insights** collection value and brand/shop breakdowns.
- Spending figures on mobile are untouched, exactly as on the web — the money went out and stays counted.
- If only part of a multi-unit purchase left, the Insights value is split proportionally. Purchases with nothing disposed report exactly the same numbers they always did.
- Mobile only reads these records; you still record a car leaving from the web app.
- Nine new tests cover the rule (`mobile/test/ownership_disposal_test.dart`). All 71 mobile tests pass.

### 12) Dashboard "Spending by Brand" is now a real chart

- It used to be a list of HTML progress bars. It is now a proper sorted bar chart, same amCharts library as the Insight page.
- **Biggest spender at the top**, always. The question you ask that panel is "which brand am I spending most on", and a chart in random order makes that a hunt.
- **Spending by Brand now takes the full width of the page**, with Recent Purchases moved below it. The two used to sit side by side at half width each, which left the bar chart squeezed and brand names cramped.
- Bars are one shade of blue, darkest for the biggest — the same "more is darker" idea as the Insight chart. Five different colours would suggest the brands are being tracked against each other over time, which they aren't.
- Each bar is labelled with the ringgit amount, and hovering shows the amount plus that brand's share of your total spending — the percentage the old list showed.
- The chart grows taller as you add brands, and the panel scrolls, so twenty brands don't get squashed into a space meant for five. Long brand names truncate on the axis and show in full in the tooltip.
- Nothing about how the numbers are worked out changed — it is the same spending-per-brand figure, drawn differently.

### 13) One shared chart component

- The Insight page's "where the cars went" chart and the new dashboard brand chart are the same thing: named categories, sorted biggest first, one hue by rank.
- Rather than two near-identical copies of the chart setup, there is now a single `SortedBarChart` used by both, with the caller passing in how to format a value (`2 cars` versus `RM 55.00`).
- Chart colours moved to `lib/chart-palette.ts` so both charts, and any future one, read from one place.
- The bars **animate into their sorted position** rather than appearing pre-sorted. The data is left in whatever order it arrived and each row is *shifted* into its ranked place, so the bars visibly settle into order as the page loads — and slide past each other rather than jumping if the ranking changes when you hit Refresh.
- The sorting follows amCharts' published sorted-bar demo closely, including the two settings that make the animation land correctly rather than over-scrolling mid-slide, and the hidden zoom-out button that would otherwise pop in while rows are moving. The one deliberate difference is colour: the demo gives every bar its own hue, while these use one hue stepped by rank, since the bars are one quantity measured across categories rather than separate series — and per-bar hues stop being tellable apart past about seven brands.
- **Hovering a bar** dims it and shows the name, the amount, and (on the dashboard) that brand's share of total spending. The brand name also appears against the axis as you move down the chart.
- A future chart worth knowing about: a horizontal bar chart here draws its first row at the **bottom**, so the values are sorted smallest-first to put the biggest bar at the top.

## User Impact

- You can finally record giving a car away, instead of choosing between deleting the purchase (losing the expense) or leaving the app saying you still own it.
- When you sell, you see what you actually made after postage and fees — not the flattering number.
- Your owned count on the Catalog and Dashboard is now honest about cars that have left — on your phone as well as the web.
- Nothing you record is permanent by accident: mistakes can be removed, and a returned parcel can be marked properly without losing the postage you spent.
- Your spending history is untouched and unchanged by any of this.

## Notes for Next Time

- **The migration is already applied** — `tbl_disposal`, both indexes and the quantity trigger are live, and the feature was tested against real data. Row Level Security is on, with both policies confirmed in the dashboard: "Authenticated full access" (ALL, authenticated) and "Public anon select" (SELECT, anon). Logged-out visitors can read disposals — which the public Catalog needs so its owned count is right — but cannot write.
- **Recording a car leaving is still web-only.** Mobile reads and subtracts them correctly, but has no screen to record one. Worth adding if you find yourself wanting to do it from your phone.
- **The Insight page is the cross-reason overview**, but it shows numbers and charts, not a combined list of records. Opening `/collection-changes` on its own redirects there. If you ever want one scrollable list mixing all five reasons together, that would be another page.
- **amCharts is used under its free "linkware" licence**, which means the small "Chart by amCharts" link must stay visible on every chart. Hiding it needs a paid licence — worth knowing before anyone tries to remove it. The licence file is in `node_modules/@amcharts/amcharts5/LICENSE`.
- Chart colours come from a validated data-visualisation palette rather than the app theme, chosen so the bars stay distinguishable for colour-blind readers and readable in both light and dark mode. The table under the charts is there so the information never depends on colour alone.
- Search and infinite scroll both work on records already loaded in the browser — the page fetches that reason's records once, then filters locally. Fine at the current volume; if the lists ever run to thousands, the filtering would need to move into the database query.
- **Delivery / shared postage was designed but not built.** When you buy 2 cars from one seller with 1 postage, or consolidate a shipment from overseas, the postage is currently typed into each car's price by hand. That works fine when you know the postage at purchase time. It breaks for overseas consolidation, where the postage only appears later and you'd have to go back and edit every row. A `tbl_delivery` table (postage split equally per car) was designed for this but deliberately left for a separate change.
- **Trades are recorded at cash value only**, with the swap written in the remark. Doing trades properly means linking two records together, which is a bigger job — better to see how often trades actually happen first.
- The quantity guard is a database trigger (`check_disposal_quantity`), so it holds even if a future screen forgets to check.
